# What is a distraction-free writing app? How to choose one

A distraction-free writing app keeps everything except your text out of the way while you write: little on screen besides your words, nothing that interrupts you, and nothing to manage mid-sentence, such as saving, syncing or formatting. To judge one, check whether the screen can be reduced to the text, whether it saves on its own and keeps earlier versions, whether it works offline, whether it has anything to scroll or reply to, how quickly it opens, and how it behaves on the device you write on.

## Where distraction comes from

Writing rarely stalls because of one big interruption; it’s usually a string of small ones:

- **Interface clutter:** toolbars, menus, tabs, and a status bar full of icons.
- **Things to read inside the app:** feeds, communities, recommendations, badges.
- **Formatting decisions:** font and color buttons that invite you to style a paragraph before it’s finished.
- **Worrying about your work:** not being sure the app has saved.
- **Lag and waiting:** a slow start, delayed keystrokes, a spinner while something syncs.
- **Switching apps:** leaving to check a name or a word, and finding everything else on the device.

## What to check in any writing app

### Can the screen be reduced to just your text?

Look for full-screen mode, or toolbars that hide while you type. On a phone, check whether the status bar can be hidden too. **Test:** type a paragraph and count what’s still on screen besides the text.

### Typewriter scrolling or focus dimming

Typewriter scrolling keeps the line you’re typing at a fixed height; focus dimming fades everything except the current sentence, line or paragraph. If you tend to reread and tinker while drafting, they’re worth a try. See [What is typewriter mode?](/en/blog/typewriter-mode/)

### Plain text or Markdown instead of a formatting toolbar

In plain text, or in Markdown, where characters such as `#` and `*` mark headings and emphasis, you keep typing instead of reaching for buttons. **Test:** make a word bold and add a heading without leaving the keyboard.

### Saving you never think about

The app should save as you type and keep earlier versions, so a bad edit isn’t permanent. **Test:** type a sentence, close the app from the app switcher right away, reopen it, then look for a version history. More in [How to never lose your writing](/en/blog/never-lose-your-draft/).

### Writing offline

Your documents should live on your device, with sync as an extra. **Test:** turn on airplane mode, write a few lines, then close and reopen the app.

### Nothing to scroll, read or reply to

A writing app doesn’t need a feed, a social layer or ads; where they exist, they compete with your text.

### Quick to open, and back where you left off

**Test:** open the app from scratch, see how long it takes before you can type, and check whether it brings back the last document at the place you stopped.

### A shortcut bar above the keyboard (phones)

Quotation marks, dashes and brackets are often several taps away on a phone keyboard. A bar of your own shortcuts above it saves those trips.

### Dark mode and adjustable type

Check for a dark appearance and controls for font size, line spacing and margins.

### Landscape and an external keyboard (phones and tablets)

Turn the device sideways: lines that run the full width of a tablet are tiring to read. If you use a keyboard, check that common shortcuts work.

## Phone, tablet or computer: what matters on each

| Device | What matters most |
|---|---|
| Phone | The keyboard covers much of the screen, so hidden bars, typewriter scrolling and a shortcut bar help most. Fast start-up matters, since you often write in short bursts. |
| Tablet | Line length in landscape, external keyboard support, and split screen if you keep notes beside your draft. |
| Computer | The text should keep a readable width in a wide window. Most interruptions come from other apps, so the system’s Do Not Disturb or Focus setting matters as much as the writing app. |

On several devices, also check that sync works without your help.

## FAQ {#faq}

### What is a distraction-free writing app?

A writing app designed to keep everything except your text out of the way: a screen that can be reduced to the words, no feeds, ads or notifications inside the app, and saving and syncing that happen without your attention.

### Is there a distraction-free writing app for Android?

Yes, many, and no single one is best for everyone. Try a few on your own phone: check for hidden bars, autosave and version history, offline writing and a quick start. Pure Writer for Android is one option; its editor, History, backups and cloud sync are free with no ads, while some features, such as preview and export, need Pure Writer Pro.

### Is there a distraction-free writing app for iPhone and iPad?

Yes, the App Store has many. On iPad, also check line length in landscape and external keyboard support. Pure Writer for iOS runs on iPhone and iPad with iOS 26 or iPadOS 26 or later, with a 7-day free trial of every feature.

### Do I need a special app, or is Do Not Disturb enough?

Do Not Disturb, or Focus on Apple devices, silences other apps and is worth turning on whatever you write with. It doesn’t change the writing app itself: its toolbars, buttons, feeds, or whether you must remember to save. Use both.

### Is typewriter mode the same as focus mode?

Not necessarily. “Focus mode” means different things in different apps; often it hides toolbars and menus. Typewriter mode works on the text itself, either keeping the line you’re typing at a fixed height or dimming everything but the current passage. In Pure Writer, it dims everything but the current paragraph.
